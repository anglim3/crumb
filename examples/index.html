Example breadboard layouts

These are the layouts that ship with Crumb, a breadboard layout tool for solderless boards. Open one to copy it into the editor. Rewiring that copy does not change the file in the repository.

Each board draws the holes, strips, and jumpers you would use at the bench, and the editor checks for shared holes and shorts. Crumb is an online breadboard diagram maker. It is not a breadboard simulator, and it will not compute LED current.

  • 555 blinker

    Astable NE555 flashing an LED

    An NE555, a 1k resistor, a 10k resistor, a 10µF capacitor, a 330Ω resistor, and an LED on a half-size board. Power and ground use the left rails.

    Open 555 blinker in the editor

  • Button LED

    Button, resistor, and LED on a mini board

    A button, a 330Ω resistor, and an LED. The button sits across the left strip and the LED returns to the ground rail.

    Open Button LED in the editor

  • LM358 amplifier

    Single-supply non-inverting stage, gain about 2

    An LM358 with two 10k resistors, a 10k potentiometer, and a 100n capacitor on a half-size board.

    Open LM358 amplifier in the editor

  • 74HC595 LED chain

    Shift register with eight LEDs

    A 74HC595 and eight LED-and-resistor pairs on a half-size board. Each LED has its own 330Ω resistor.

    Open 74HC595 LED chain in the editor

  • ATtiny85 blink

    Reset pull-up and an LED on PB0

    An ATtiny85, a 10k reset pull-up, a 330Ω resistor, and an LED on a half-size board.

    Open ATtiny85 blink in the editor

  • ESP32 LED

    30-pin ESP32 DevKit and an LED on IO2

    The 30-pin DevKit, a 330Ω resistor, and an LED on a half-size board. This part is not a 38-pin DevKitC.

    Open ESP32 LED in the editor

  • Pico button

    Pico with a pulled-up button

    A Pico, a button, and a 10k pull-up on a half-size board.

    Open Pico button in the editor

  • Uno + DHT22

    Uno module and a DHT22

    An Uno module beside the board, a DHT22 on the right strip, and a 10k pull-up, on a half-size board.

    Open Uno + DHT22 in the editor

  • HomeKit blinds

    Nano ESP32, TMC2208, NEMA 17, and a limit switch

    A Nano ESP32, a TMC2208, a limit switch, a barrel jack, and a NEMA 17 on a half-size board. The jack feeds the lower right rail, and that rail is not jumped across the middle split. The left positive rail is 3V3 from the Nano ESP32 and is jumped across the split so the driver can share it.

    Open HomeKit blinds in the editor
