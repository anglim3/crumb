How it works

Crumb is a breadboard layout tool. It draws the solderless board you will build: the holes, the strips, and the jumpers. It checks that two parts do not share a hole and that power does not meet ground. It does not simulate the circuit. If you were looking for a breadboard simulator, this is the diagram of the board, not a model of the voltages.

The editor runs in the browser. Layouts stay in this browser until you save them into its library or copy a share link. There is no account and no cloud copy.

Placing parts

Open the editor and pick a part from the list. A click, or Enter on a keyboard, starts placement. The status line tells you which hole comes next.

A DIP chip needs one click: the hole for pin 1. Pin 1 sits on the left strip, column e, and the body crosses the gutter. The Nano ESP32 is the exception — its pin 1 is D12 and sits in column f. nano and nano-esp32 are different boards. The ESP32 part is the 30-pin DevKit, not a 38-pin DevKitC.

A leaded part needs a click for every lead. A resistor or LED uses two holes. A three-pin part also needs the middle hole. A four-pin part needs one more. Hole addresses look like 10-e for row 10, column e.

With Select active, drag a part to move it by rows. Delete removes the selected part or wire. Undo is Ctrl Z, or Command Z on a Mac. Press ? in the editor for the full shortcut list.

Wiring

Choose Wire, or press W. Click the start hole, then the end hole. A second click on the start hole cancels that jumper. You can also start a wire from a placed pin such as u1.VCC or an off-board module pin such as m1.pos.

Columns a–e on a row are already one strip. Columns f–j are another. The gutter between e and f keeps them apart, so a jumper is what joins the two sides. Hover a hole to light every hole on its net.

LP and LM are the left positive and ground rails. RP and RM are the right rails. Power jumpers are red. Ground jumpers are black. Half-size and full-size boards split each rail at the middle. If the two halves should be the same net, add a jumper across the split. Mini boards do not split the rails.

A short is marked when a positive rail meets a ground rail, when a part’s supply pin and ground pin land on the same net, or when both leads of a resistor, LED, or similar part sit on one strip. Tying two signal pins together on purpose, such as a timer’s trigger and threshold, is not treated as a short.

Sharing a link

Copy link puts the whole layout in the address, after #c=. Send that URL to someone else and their editor opens the same board.

Opening a share link does not replace the projects already saved in that browser. The shared board is a view until you save it. Save writes it into this browser’s library. There is no live collaboration: the link is a copy of the layout at the moment it was copied.

Export

The editor can download the layout as JSON, SVG, or PNG. JSON is the project file: the board size, the parts, and the wires. SVG is the drawing of that board. PNG is a picture of the same drawing.

The command-line renderer writes that same SVG from a project file, with no install. The files under examples/ are known-good layouts in that JSON shape. The examples page opens each of them in the editor.
